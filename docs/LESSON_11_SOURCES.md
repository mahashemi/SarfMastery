## Six second-person slots

| # | Addressee | Imperative | Prohibitive |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | أَنْتَ | اُكْتُبْ | لَا تَكْتُبْ |
| 2 | أَنْتُمَا (masculine dual) | اُكْتُبَا | لَا تَكْتُبَا |
| 3 | أَنْتُمْ | اُكْتُبُوا | لَا تَكْتُبُوا |
| 4 | أَنْتِ | اُكْتُبِي | لَا تَكْتُبِي |
| 5 | أَنْتُمَا (feminine dual) | اُكْتُبَا | لَا تَكْتُبَا |
| 6 | أَنْتُنَّ | اُكْتُبْنَ | لَا تَكْتُبْنَ |

## Pedagogical safeguards

1. Direct imperative and prohibitive forms address the second person. Do not create a 14-form table for them.
2. Keep six traditional gender/number slots, but explain that the two dual slots share the same surface forms; there are five distinct surface forms.
3. The imperative is built from the second-person jussive muḍāriʿ, not by simply adding لَا to the command.
4. The prohibitive is لَا النَّاهِيَةُ + jussive muḍāriʿ.
5. For this example, the jussive drops the nūn from dual, masculine plural, and feminine singular endings: تَكْتُبَانِ → لَا تَكْتُبَا; تَكْتُبُونَ → لَا تَكْتُبُوا; تَكْتُبِينَ → لَا تَكْتُبِي. The feminine plural nūn remains in تَكْتُبْنَ.
6. The lesson uses the sound Form I verb كَتَبَ. Weak, hamzated, doubled, and augmented verbs may need additional rules.
7. Indirect commands and prohibitions (third person or first person plural) use other constructions and are not covered here.
8. No page references are assigned to Mīzān uṣ-Ṣarf, Mīzān wa Munshaʿib, or Dānesh-e Ṣarf until exact editions are identified and inspected.
