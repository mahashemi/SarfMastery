# Product Specification

## Identity and audience
- Product: **Sarf Mastery — صَرْف**.
- No personal-name branding or personal signature anywhere in the app.
- Primary audience: absolute beginners progressing toward Hawza-level Arabic grammar.
- Core variety: clear الفصحى, with classical/Qur'anic language introduced progressively and labelled.
- Daily target: 20–30 minutes.

## Learning destination
1. Acquire usable Arabic through listening, speaking, reading, and writing.
2. Read graded Arabic with progressively less tashkīl and transliteration.
3. Understand short connected passages and express familiar ideas.
4. Follow Arabic classroom instructions and simple explanations.
5. Learn formal النَّحْوُ and الصَّرْفُ primarily in Arabic.
6. Progress to traditional Hawza texts and careful analysis of authentic material.

## Curriculum sequence
Optional script-literacy bridge → functional beginner Arabic → connected Arabic → Arabic as the language of learning → Arabic-medium grammar → traditional Hawza study.

The detailed governing plan is [LANGUAGE_FIRST_CURRICULUM.md](LANGUAGE_FIRST_CURRICULUM.md). The former 60-day Sarf-first sequence is superseded as the learner entry path. Existing Sarf lessons are draft source material for the later grammar stage.

## Pedagogy
Learn through meaningful language before formalising grammar. Use discovery, comparison, prediction, and communication. Introduce technical terms only when they help describe patterns the learner has already encountered. At the grammar stage, teach definitions and exercises in accessible Arabic first; keep English, Persian, and Urdu as optional scaffolds.

## Language requirements
- All four skills—listening, speaking, reading, writing—must appear across each week.
- Teach useful phrases, collocations, and high-frequency vocabulary in context.
- Use full tashkīl in early reading and pronunciation/morphology examples.
- Transliteration is temporary and should fade with reading ability.
- Distinguish contemporary MSA, classical Arabic, and dialect examples.
- Verified human audio is preferred; never claim browser TTS is verified human speech.
- Persian and Urdu are optional bridge languages; translations require fluent review.

## Readiness and assessment
Use evidence from listening, speaking, reading, writing, vocabulary recall, comprehension of Arabic instructions, and ability to explain a familiar pattern. Do not unlock Arabic-medium grammar based only on elapsed time or a multiple-choice score. The readiness gate is described in the governing curriculum.

## Responsive UX
- Desktop: spacious reading/listening workspace, transcripts and exercises side-by-side where useful.
- Tablet: adaptive columns and touch-friendly controls.
- Mobile: single-column learning flow, no unintended horizontal overflow.
- Arabic RTL, English LTR, Persian/Urdu RTL, with reliable tashkīl positioning and mixed-direction handling.

## Progress and privacy
First version works without an account. Keep progress on-device. Distinguish vocabulary recognition from active recall and skill-specific performance. Add export/import before considering cloud sync.

## Integrity
Verify Arabic content with competent review before marking it verified. Verify Qur'an and hadith references and attribution. Never invent audio provenance, textbook pages, or scholarly consensus. Keep morphology distinct from tafsir. Write original explanations and exercises; do not reproduce protected textbook chapters.
