# Language-First Arabic Curriculum — Sarf Mastery

**Decision status:** Adopted curriculum direction; replaces the previous assumption that a learner should begin with formal Sarf.
**Product:** Sarf Mastery — صَرْف
**Daily target:** 20–30 minutes, with optional immersion outside the core session.
**Primary Arabic variety:** clear Modern Standard Arabic (الفُصْحَى الْمُعَاصِرَةُ), with carefully selected classical/Qur'anic language introduced progressively. This is a bridge to Hawza and classical study, not a dialect course.
**Teaching principle:** acquire usable Arabic first; learn to discuss its grammar in Arabic once the learner has enough Arabic to understand the explanations.

---

## 1. The revised destination

The learner should ultimately be able to:
1. Understand and use simple, useful Arabic in listening, speaking, reading, and writing.
2. Read fully vowelled beginner material with increasingly less transliteration.
3. Understand short connected passages without translating every word.
4. Describe familiar language patterns in simple Arabic.
5. Study النَّحْوُ and الصَّرْفُ through Arabic definitions, instructions, examples, questions, and exercises.
6. Progress into traditional Hawza texts and analyse Qur'anic/classical examples with scholarly care.

The course is therefore a **language course first and a grammar course second**. The existing 19 Sarf lesson drafts become a later reference bank; they are not the learner's starting sequence.

## 2. Why this sequence is better

A learner who cannot yet understand ordinary Arabic explanations is forced to learn two things simultaneously: the grammatical concept and the language used to explain it. This creates unnecessary cognitive load and encourages memorising labels without a usable language foundation.

Instead:
- First build meaning, vocabulary, listening comprehension, sentence intuition, and reading fluency.
- Let learners notice recurring forms naturally. Do not pretend grammar is absent; simply delay technical analysis.
- Introduce a small amount of practical language about language (for example, اِسْمٌ and فِعْلٌ only when useful), without launching formal paradigms.
- When the learner can follow simple Arabic instructions and explanations, switch the grammar classroom language progressively to Arabic.
- Keep English, Persian, and Urdu as temporary scaffolds that can be revealed when needed, not as the permanent medium of grammar instruction.

## 3. Language and content policy

### Arabic variety
- Teach clear, broadly understood الفصحى as the core language.
- Use contemporary, natural sentences rather than artificial sentences created only to demonstrate a rule.
- Gradually add classical vocabulary, Qur'anic expressions, and selected heritage texts.
- Clearly label Qur'anic/classical wording when it differs from contemporary everyday usage.
- Do not mix dialect forms into the core course without an explicit label and reason.

### Script, pronunciation, and tashkīl
- If the learner cannot confidently decode Arabic script, start with a short literacy bridge: letter shapes, joining, short vowels, sukūn, shaddah, tanwīn, and long vowels.
- Use full tashkīl in early reading and all examples where pronunciation or morphology is being taught.
- Transliteration is temporary support and should fade as decoding improves.
- Audio must be authentic and quality-checked. Provide natural and slower versions where licensed recordings exist; never represent browser TTS as verified human pronunciation.

### Four skills
Every week must include listening, speaking, reading, and writing. Receptive skills (listening/reading) feed productive skills (speaking/writing). Grammar is not a substitute for communication.

### Vocabulary
- Prioritise high-frequency, reusable words and phrases.
- Teach words in collocations and sentences, not isolated lists alone.
- Recycle vocabulary in spaced intervals and across different contexts.
- Track recognition separately from active recall and productive use.
- Include useful classroom language early: اِسْمَعْ، اِقْرَأْ، أَعِدْ، اُكْتُبْ، اِخْتَرْ، صِلْ، أَجِبْ.

## 4. Staged pathway and readiness gates

These are instructional stages inspired by CEFR-style descriptors, not a claim of official CEFR certification. Calendar time is an estimate; advancement depends on demonstrated ability, not a fixed number of days.

### Stage 0 — Arabic literacy bridge (only if needed)
**Purpose:** make the script usable rather than assuming the learner can already read it.

Teach:
- Letter recognition and positional forms.
- Joining and word segmentation.
- Fatḥah, kasrah, ḍammah, sukūn, shaddah, tanwīn.
- Short vs long vowels.
- Hamzah and common reading conventions.
- Reading syllables, familiar words, and short fully vowelled phrases aloud.

**Exit evidence:** accurately decode a short, fully vowelled beginner passage with reasonable sound-letter correspondence. Do not require advanced tajwīd; Qur'an recitation rules are a separate discipline.

### Stage 1 — Functional beginner Arabic (A1-like)
**Purpose:** understand and use short, predictable language about familiar life.

Content:
- Greetings, introductions, family, home, study, time, numbers, food, places, daily routines, and basic needs.
- High-frequency nouns, verbs, adjectives, pronouns, question words, prepositions, and connectors in meaningful phrases.
- Listening to short dialogues and classroom instructions.
- Asking and answering simple questions.
- Reading short dialogues, captions, descriptions, and graded mini-stories.
- Writing words, short answers, and simple connected sentences.
- Natural exposure to past, present, command, gender, number, and agreement through examples—without full Sarf tables or technical derivation.

**Exit evidence:** understand familiar classroom instructions; introduce oneself; ask and answer predictable questions; read a short familiar passage; write a connected short description; understand the main point of slow, clear speech on familiar topics.

### Stage 2 — Connected Arabic (A2-like)
**Purpose:** move beyond memorised phrases into connected comprehension and expression.

Content:
- Daily life, education, travel, health, community, weather, plans, experiences, opinions, and simple explanations.
- Short narratives in past and present; descriptions; requests; directions; comparisons; reasons and preferences.
- Common connectors and discourse markers: وَ، فَ، ثُمَّ، لَكِنْ، لِأَنَّ، لِذٰلِكَ، بَعْدَ ذٰلِكَ.
- Reading graded stories, short informational passages, simple biographies, and carefully selected Islamic educational prose.
- Retelling a short story, describing an event, asking follow-up questions, and writing a short paragraph.
- Notice recurring word families and sentence patterns; introduce simple metalanguage only when it clarifies what the learner already understands.

**Exit evidence:** follow the main idea and key details of clear, short speech; read connected passages with limited dictionary support; narrate a familiar event; describe and compare; write a coherent paragraph; follow simple Arabic-only lesson instructions and answer comprehension questions in Arabic.

### Stage 3 — Arabic as the language of learning (bridge to grammar)
**Purpose:** prepare the learner to study grammar *through Arabic*.

Content:
- Classroom and academic Arabic: مَعْنَى، مِثَال، سَبَب، فَرْق، صَحِيح، خَطَأ، أَكْمِلْ، اِسْتَخْرِجْ، عَيِّنْ، عَلِّلْ، قَارِنْ، صَرِّفْ.
- Explain a familiar word, phrase, or sentence in simple Arabic.
- Read short Arabic explanations with glossaries, diagrams, and examples.
- Ask for clarification in Arabic and paraphrase an instruction.
- Build a controlled grammar vocabulary without yet overwhelming the learner with classical terminology.
- Transition from bilingual explanations to Arabic-first explanations; keep a revealable support translation.

**Readiness gate for Arabic-medium grammar:** learner can understand the common instructions used in lessons; follow a short, simple Arabic explanation supported by examples; read a short vowelled passage independently; answer basic comprehension questions in Arabic; and explain a familiar language pattern in simple Arabic. A quiz score alone is insufficient.

### Stage 4 — Grammar taught in Arabic
**Purpose:** study formal grammar with Arabic as the primary teaching language.

Start with a gentle Arabic-medium orientation:
- مَا النَّحْوُ؟ وَمَا الصَّرْفُ؟
- مَا الْكَلِمَةُ؟ وَمَا أَنْوَاعُهَا؟
- مَا الْجِذْرُ؟ وَمَا الْوَزْنُ؟
- مَا الْفِعْلُ؟ وَمَا الْمَصْدَرُ؟

Then follow a carefully scaffolded sequence:
1. Word categories and simple sentence observation.
2. Basic sentence roles and sentence types (a controlled Nahw bridge).
3. Root and pattern; فَاءُ الْفِعْلِ، عَيْنُ الْفِعْلِ، لَامُ الْفِعْلِ.
4. Past and present forms through meaningful sentences.
5. Pronouns and the fourteen ṣīghahs.
6. Active/passive and imperative/prohibitive.
7. Six common Abwāb.
8. Mujarrad and Mazīd.
9. Maṣdar and derived nouns.
10. Sound, hamzated, doubled, and weak verbs.
11. Iʿlāl, Ibdāl, and Idghām.
12. Guided reading and analysis of verified Qur'anic, hadith, and classical examples.

**Arabic-medium rule:** define in simple Arabic first, show examples, ask comprehension questions in Arabic, and invite the learner to explain in Arabic. English/Persian/Urdu explanations remain available as optional support for difficult ideas, not the default lesson voice.

### Stage 5 — Traditional Hawza study
- Align formal terminology and order with the learner's selected Hawza texts and teacher.
- Compare textbook conventions rather than assuming all texts organise material identically.
- Read original Arabic definitions and paradigms.
- Practise full paradigms, derivation, parsing, and unseen-word analysis.
- Use Qur'an, hadith, and classical prose with reliable source verification.
- Keep linguistic analysis distinct from tafsīr, legal inference, and hadith authentication.

## 5. The daily learning loop (20–30 minutes)

A standard session should feel like a language lesson, not a grammar lecture:

1. **Retrieve (3 min):** recall yesterday's phrases and a few older words.
2. **Listen (4 min):** hear a short dialogue or passage; first for gist, then for detail.
3. **Understand (4 min):** match meaning, images, actions, or comprehension questions.
4. **Notice (3 min):** compare useful expressions or word forms; make a prediction.
5. **Speak (4 min):** repeat, substitute, answer, or record a short response.
6. **Read/write (4 min):** read the same language in text and produce a short response.
7. **Review (3 min):** active vocabulary recall and a scheduled review.

Not every skill needs equal time every day, but all four skills must appear across the week. On selected days, extend the session for a story, speaking task, or writing task.

## 6. The first 12-week language foundation (provisional scope)

This is a course map, not a promise that every learner will reach a fixed proficiency in 12 weeks. Pace should respond to mastery evidence.

| Week | Communicative theme | Language functions | Reading/listening outcome | Productive outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greetings and classroom | greet, introduce, follow instructions | understand short greetings and class commands | introduce self; respond to simple commands |
| 2 | People and family | identify, ask who, describe simply | understand a family dialogue | describe family members |
| 3 | Home and objects | locate, name, describe | understand a simple room description | describe a room and where things are |
| 4 | Daily routine | say what one does and when | follow a short daily-routine account | describe a typical day |
| 5 | Study and learning | ask, answer, request, clarify | follow a classroom exchange | talk about study and ask for help |
| 6 | Food and needs | request, prefer, accept/refuse | understand a simple ordering dialogue | express needs and preferences |
| 7 | Places and directions | locate, ask where, give simple directions | understand a route or place description | ask for and give directions |
| 8 | Time and plans | tell time, arrange, say when | understand a simple schedule | describe plans |
| 9 | Past experiences | say what happened, sequence events | follow a short familiar narrative | retell a simple event |
| 10 | Description and comparison | describe, compare, qualify | understand short descriptive passages | compare people, places, and objects |
| 11 | Reasons and opinions | give a reason, agree/disagree simply | understand a short opinion with clear reasons | state an opinion and explain why |
| 12 | Integrated project | combine skills and repair communication | understand a graded passage/dialogue | deliver a short talk and write a paragraph |

Each week must include a vocabulary set, useful chunks, listening material, pronunciation focus, reading, speaking task, writing task, retrieval practice, and a short mastery check. The exact lexical syllabus must be developed from frequency and usefulness, not improvised as isolated word lists.

## 7. How natural pattern learning fits without becoming hidden grammar

During Stages 1–2:
- Learners repeatedly hear and use forms such as كَتَبَ، يَكْتُبُ، كَتَبْتُ، كَاتِبٌ in meaningful contexts when appropriate.
- They notice who is acting, when something happened, and how a word changes.
- They practise the forms as useful language chunks and sentence frames.
- They are not asked to memorise the 14 ṣīghahs, the six Abwāb, or technical iʿlāl rules prematurely.
- Occasional simple observations are allowed: “When I say أَنَا, I hear أَكْتُبُ”; the formal label can wait.

At Stage 3, the learner learns how to talk *about* language. At Stage 4, the formal Sarf sequence becomes explicit and rigorous.

## 8. Assessment and progression

Assess what the learner can actually do, not merely what they can recognise on a multiple-choice quiz.

For each stage, gather evidence in:
- Listening comprehension.
- Spoken interaction and short monologue.
- Reading comprehension.
- Writing.
- Vocabulary recognition and active retrieval.
- Ability to follow Arabic instructions.
- Ability to explain a familiar pattern.

Use a mix of short checks, recorded or live speaking samples, reading aloud, retelling, and writing. A stage is completed after consistent evidence across multiple sessions and task types. Keep a learner profile of strengths and gaps; do not block all progress because of one weak skill, but do not claim readiness for Arabic-medium grammar when comprehension is not yet adequate.

## 9. Application redesign

The app should become a staged Arabic-learning environment, not a dashboard that immediately presents formal Sarf units.

### Primary navigation
- **Learn:** guided language lessons and later formal grammar.
- **Listen:** dialogues, short passages, repeat and comprehension.
- **Speak:** prompts, substitution drills, role-play, optional recording.
- **Read:** graded texts with progressive supports.
- **Write:** guided sentence and paragraph production.
- **Words:** searchable vocabulary, phrases, collocations, and review.
- **Progress:** skill-specific mastery and next recommended activity.
- **Grammar:** locked or preview-only until the readiness gate is met; do not mislead the learner into thinking it is the starting point.

### Lesson template for language stage
1. Goal stated as “I can…” in plain English and simple Arabic as soon as possible.
2. Useful phrases in context.
3. Audio and meaning check.
4. Vocabulary and collocations.
5. Guided noticing.
6. Pronunciation and speaking.
7. Reading.
8. Short writing.
9. Retrieval and spaced review.
10. A real-world task or mini-dialogue.
11. Feedback and next-step recommendation.

### Lesson template for Arabic-medium grammar
1. تمهيدٌ / warm-up in Arabic.
2. أمثلةٌ / examples before definition.
3. اِكْتَشِفْ / guided observation.
4. التَّعْرِيفُ / concise Arabic definition.
5. الشَّرْحُ / stepwise explanation in accessible Arabic.
6. تَطْبِيقٌ / worked example.
7. تَمْرِينٌ / guided and independent practice.
8. تَقْوِيمٌ / comprehension and production check.
9. خُلَاصَةٌ / learner summary in Arabic.
10. مُرَاجَعَةٌ / spaced review.

## 10. Content-quality requirements

- Every Arabic sentence must be natural and checked by a competent Arabic reviewer before being marked verified.
- Fully vowel early-stage texts and target examples; gradually remove tashkīl only after reading readiness is demonstrated.
- Record audio only from a verified speaker with permission and clear licensing.
- Keep transliteration consistent and progressively optional.
- Teach grammar through meaning and use before formal analysis.
- Use authentic, age-appropriate, culturally respectful contexts.
- Avoid artificial or misleading examples written solely to fit a rule.
- Distinguish MSA, classical Arabic, and any dialect examples.
- Provide accurate English support; Persian/Urdu remain optional bridge languages and require fluent review.
- No invented source, audio, hadith, Qur'anic reference, or textbook citation.
- Never claim formal certification or CEFR level based only on in-app quiz results.

## 12. Superseded plan

The previous 60-day plan that begins with formal Sarf and introduces the Mīzān and fourteen ṣīghahs in the first weeks is **not the learner-facing course sequence anymore**. Its topics are retained as a later grammar syllabus and as source material. Do not continue adding Lesson 20 as the next learner lesson. The next content work is the diagnostic, literacy bridge, and Week 1 functional Arabic.
