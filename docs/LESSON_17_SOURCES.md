## Claim-to-source map

| Claim | Supporting source | Scope / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| A weak verb (فِعْلٌ مُعْتَلٌّ) contains a weak radical, traditionally represented by و or ي, with ا often appearing as a surface long-vowel outcome. | *Arabic: An Essential Grammar*, sections on weak verbs; *Basic Arabic: A Grammar and Workbook*, weak verb paradigms. | Do not infer the underlying root solely from the surface alif; consult the paradigm/dictionary. |

## Pedagogical safeguards
1. Teach root analysis before surface spelling. The alif in قَالَ represents the outcome of a weak radical in the paradigm; it is not simply the third radical.
2. This lesson classifies roots and does not claim to cover all rules of إِعْلَالٌ, deletion, hamza, or orthography.
3. Distinguish Lafīf mafrūq from maqrūn by whether the two weak radicals are separated or adjacent.
4. Do not imply all verbs containing alif are weak in the same way; verify the underlying root.
5. No textbook page references are assigned until the exact Hawza textbook editions are identified and inspected.
