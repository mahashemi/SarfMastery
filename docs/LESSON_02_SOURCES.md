## Lesson examples

- اِسْمٌ — *ism*: كِتَابٌ (kitābun, a book), بَيْتٌ (baytun, a house), مَاءٌ (māʾun, water).
- فِعْلٌ — *fiʿl*: كَتَبَ (kataba, he wrote), يَكْتُبُ (yaktubu, he writes), اُكْتُبْ (uktub, write!).
- حَرْفٌ — *ḥarf*: فِي (fī, in), مِنْ (min, from), إِلَى (ilā, to/towards).

These are ordinary teaching examples, not quotations from the Qur'an or hadith.

## Pedagogical safeguards

1. In this grammatical classification, حَرْفٌ means a particle/function word, not an alphabet character.
2. اِسْمٌ is broader than a proper name; it includes many categories that English grammar may label separately.
3. English and Arabic part-of-speech systems should not be mapped one-to-one without explanation.
4. The definitions “ism is not tied to time,” “fiʿl is connected with time,” and “ḥarf functions through relation to other words” are introductory descriptions. Later lessons will teach the identifying signs and more precise traditional formulations.
5. The imperative اُكْتُبْ is a command form; do not describe it as a simple English-style present or past tense.
6. No textbook page references are assigned until the exact selected editions are identified and inspected.
