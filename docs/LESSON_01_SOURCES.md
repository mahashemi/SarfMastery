# Lesson 1 — Source and verification notes

Last reviewed: 2026-11-05

This file records the public linguistic references used to prepare the introductory lesson. These sources support general linguistic explanations; they do **not** replace the selected Hawza textbooks, and no textbook page numbers are claimed here.

## Example forms in the lesson

- كَتَبَ — *kataba* — he wrote.
- يَكْتُبُ — *yaktubu* — he writes.
- كَاتِبٌ — *kātibun* — a writer / scribe (active participle).
- مَكْتُوبٌ — *maktūbun* — written (passive participle).
- كِتَابَةٌ — *kitābatun* — writing.
- مَكْتَبٌ — *maktabun* — office / desk, depending on context.
- كِتَابٌ — *kitābun* — a book.

The forms are presented in an indefinite nominative citation form where appropriate. A word's case, definiteness, and contextual meaning can change its ending or translation. These examples are for introductory morphology and are not Qur'anic quotations.

## Pedagogical safeguards

1. A root is not itself a complete word or a one-to-one English translation.
2. Related words share a lexical relationship, but their meanings are not interchangeable.
3. A pattern is a useful structural template; its contribution is not always a simple predictable gloss.
4. The lesson's Sarf/Naḥw contrast is an introductory distinction, not a claim that morphology and syntax never interact.
5. No Qur'anic verse is quoted as an example in this lesson. The Quranic Arabic Corpus is used as a linguistic reference, not as tafsir.
6. No page/chapter references are assigned to Mīzān uṣ-Ṣarf, Mīzān wa Munshaʿib, or Dānesh-e Ṣarf until exact editions are identified and checked.
