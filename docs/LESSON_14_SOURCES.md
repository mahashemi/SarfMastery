| Lesson claim | Supporting source | Scope / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| A maṣdar (مَصْدَر) is a verbal noun that names the action or event, rather than a verb marked for person and tense. | *Arabic: An Essential Grammar*, section on the verbal noun; [Langrit — Verbal nouns (المصدر)](https://langrit.com/learn/arabic-grammar/masdar). | The lesson uses the beginner label “action noun”; a maṣdar can function in different syntactic positions like other nouns. |

## Pedagogical safeguards

1. The Form I maṣdar is سماعي in the traditional teaching sense: it has many patterns and should be learned with the verb.
2. Forms II–X have more recognisable patterns, but “regular” does not mean that every root has a verb in every form or that every maṣdar is mechanically predictable.
3. Forms II and III may have more than one maṣdar pattern; do not force one pattern onto every verb.
4. The lesson distinguishes maṣdar (action), ism fāʿil (doer), and ism mafʿūl (affected person/thing). These are separate noun categories.
5. The ك ت ب family examples are كِتَابَةٌ (writing), كَاتِبٌ (writer), and مَكْتُوبٌ (written).
6. No textbook page references are assigned to Mīzān uṣ-Ṣarf, Mīzān wa Munshaʿib, or Dānesh-e Ṣarf until exact editions are identified and inspected.
