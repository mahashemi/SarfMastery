## Lesson examples

- كَتَبَ — *kataba* — he wrote.
- يَكْتُبُ — *yaktubu* — he writes / is writing.
- اُكْتُبْ — *uktub* — write! (imperative).
- كِتَابَةٌ — *kitābatun* — writing (maṣdar).
- قَرَأَ — *qaraʾa* — he read.
- يَقْرَأُ — *yaqraʾu* — he reads.
- قِرَاءَةٌ — *qirāʾatun* — reading (maṣdar).
- فَهْمٌ — *fahmun* — understanding (maṣdar).
- كِتَابٌ — *kitābun* — a book; an ism, not the maṣdar of كَتَبَ.

These are ordinary language examples, not Qur'anic quotations.

## Pedagogical safeguards

1. The maṣdar is a noun that names an action/event; do not call it a fourth verb tense.
2. A maṣdar does not specify the time of the event by itself. Context supplies temporal interpretation.
3. A maṣdar is an ism, so it follows nominal grammar; later lessons will cover its case and definiteness in context.
4. Do not imply that every Form I maṣdar follows one productive pattern. The learner should memorise an attested maṣdar with its verb.
5. The imperative اُكْتُبْ is a command form. Do not flatten it into an English-style tense label.
6. The forms shown are citation examples; case endings and definiteness can change in sentences.
7. No textbook page references are assigned to Mīzān uṣ-Ṣarf, Mīzān wa Munshaʿib, or Dānesh-e Ṣarf until exact editions are identified and inspected.

## Remaining review

- Have a qualified Arabic/Sarf teacher review the forms, vowel marks, transliteration, and traditional definitions.
- Have fluent Persian and Urdu readers review the translations.
- Expand the lesson only after the distinction between fiʿl, maṣdar, and other ism is firmly understood.
